# Camera, direction, transition

The **CAM** section at the top of the timeline holds camera work and scene changes. From the top: camera, transition, direction.

![](img/timeline-cut2.png)

## Camera

- Keys set the camera's **Position**, **Scale** (zoom) and **Rotation**. Open the row and the three become lanes of their own, each with its own keys. The canvas, playback and export move by them.
- Keys sit only inside the cut's frames. Change the cut's length and the last key follows the end.
- Select a key and press the [Edit button](/en/timeline?id=the-edit-button) to set its name, the speed at **In** and **Out** (**Linear**, **Ease**, **Fast**, **Smooth**, **Hold**) and the **Influence**. The key's mark takes that shape.
- A position key's **Path** is **Straight** or **Curved**. A curved path is bent with handles on the canvas. Turn on **Roving** and the key's time is freed: it passes between the keys before and after at an even speed (the first and last keys keep their time).
- The camera row's **Graph** button shows the **Value** and **Speed** graphs.
- The **Camera path** button at the end of the name field draws the camera's path on the canvas.
- The eye on the camera row turns the camera view on and off; the bar beside it sets how dark the outside of the frame is.
- Zoom in and **T.U** is written on the camera row by itself; zoom out, **T.B**; any other move, **PAN**. They are written onto the timesheet too.

## Transition

- Scene changes such as FI, FO, WI, WO and O.L. The kind is changed with the [Edit button](/en/timeline?id=the-edit-button), the length by dragging the block's end. The canvas, playback and export show them.
- O.L spans two cuts, so it is edited on the [conte](/en/storyboard) panel.

## Direction

- Instructions such as PAN, T.U and T.B, written as blocks. They are notation for the sheet and move nothing on screen.
- You can draw on the direction row too.

All three are written into the CAM column of the [timesheet](/en/sheets) at once.
